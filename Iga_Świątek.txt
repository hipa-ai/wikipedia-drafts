'''Iga Natalia Świątek'''{{efn|{{IPA|pl|ˈiɡa naˈtalja ˈɕfjɔntɛk|-|LL-Q809 (pol)-Ptjackyll-Iga Świątek.wav}}
<ref name="saying-name">{{cite web |title=Swiatek teaches everyone how to say her name | url=https://www.tennis.com/baseline/articles/swiatek-teaches-everyone-how-to-say-her-name |website=Tennis.com |access-date=28 July 2025 |archive-date=28 July 2025 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20250728113417/https://www.tennis.com/baseline/articles/swiatek-teaches-everyone-how-to-say-her-name |url-status=live}}</ref>}} (born 31 May 2001) is a Polish professional [[tennis]] player. Currently ranked world No. 3 in women's singles by the [[WTA rankings|WTA]], she has held the world [[List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players|No. 1 ranking]] for a total of 125 weeks ([[List of WTA number 1 ranked singles tennis players#Weeks at No. 1|seventh-most of all-time]]). Świątek has won 25
[[WTA Tour]]–level singles titles, including six [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] titles: four at the [[French Open]], one at [[Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]], and one at the [[US Open (tennis)|US Open]]. She has also won the [[2023 WTA Finals – Singles|2023 WTA Finals]] and eleven [[WTA 1000]] titles. Świątek is the first Pole to win a major singles title.

As a junior, Świątek was the [[2018 French Open – Girls' doubles|2018 French Open]] girls' doubles champion alongside [[Caty McNally]] and the [[2018 Wimbledon Championships – Girls' singles|2018 Wimbledon]] girls' singles champion. She began playing regularly on the WTA Tour in 2019, and entered the top 50 at 18 years old after her first Tour final and a fourth-round appearance at the [[2019 French Open – Women's singles|2019 French Open]]. In [[2020 French Open – Women's singles|2020]], Świątek won her first major at the French Open in dominant fashion, losing no more than five games in any singles match. She entered the top ten of the WTA rankings for the first time in May 2021.

In early 2022, Świątek surged into dominance with a 37-match winning streak, the longest on the WTA Tour in the 21st century, becoming world No. 1 in the process. With major titles at the [[2022 French Open – Women's singles|French]] and [[2022 US Open – Women's singles|US Open]]s, she finished 2022 as the world's best player. She repeated the year-end No. 1 feat in 2023 by defending her [[2023 French Open – Women's singles|French Open]] title and claiming the WTA Finals, and won the French Open for a third straight edition in [[2024 French Open – Women's singles|2024]]. Świątek won her first [[grass court]] title at the [[2025 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|2025 Wimbledon Championships]]. She has claimed the French Open title at four of her seven appearances at the tournament, having never lost a match before the fourth round.

Świątek has an all-court playing style. She won the [[WTA Fan Favorite Awards#WTA Shot of the Year|WTA Fan Favorite Shot of the Year]] in 2019 with a [[drop shot]] from the baseline, and was voted [[WTA Fan Favorite Awards#Fan Favorite Singles Player of the Year|WTA Fan Favorite Singles Player of the Year]] in 2020. In 2023, she was named [[L'Équipe Champion of Champions|''L'Équipe'' Champion of Champions]] and [[Polish Sports Personality of the Year]] and included on ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]''{{'}}s annual list of the [[Time 100|100 most influential people in the world]].

==Junior years==
[[File:Iga Swiatek – Wimbledon junior champion 2018 (50497948673) (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|upright=.7|Świątek with the [[2018 Wimbledon Championships – Girls' singles|2018 Wimbledon junior champion's trophy]]]]
Świątek reached a career-high ranking of No. 5 as a junior player. She began competing in [[ITF Junior Circuit]] events in 2015 and won back-to-back low-level Grade 4 titles in April and May at age 13. Before the end of the year, she moved up to Grade 2 events and finished runner-up in both singles and doubles at the Czech Junior Open. Świątek made her junior Grand Slam tournament debut in 2016 at the [[2016 French Open|French Open]], reaching the quarterfinals in both singles and doubles. She followed this up with her best junior title to date at the Grade 1 Canadian Open Junior Championships, defeating [[Olga Danilović]] in the final.<ref name="itf-junior-singles">{{cite web |title=Iga Swiatek Junior Singles Activity |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/iga-swiatek/800375475/pol/jt/s/activity/ |website=ITF Tennis |access-date=4 October 2020}}</ref><ref name="itf-junior-doubles">{{cite web |title=Iga Swiatek Junior Doubles Activity |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/iga-swiatek/800375475/pol/jt/d/activity/ |website=ITF Tennis |access-date=4 October 2020 |archive-date=3 March 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210303070216/https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/iga-swiatek/800375475/pol/jt/d/activity/ |url-status=live}}</ref>

==Professional==
===2016–2018: Seven ITF Circuit championships===
[[File:YOGBA 2018 - Tennis - Women's Doubles Final 19 (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=.7|Świątek at the [[2018 Summer Youth Olympics]]]]
Świątek began competing on the [[ITF Women's Circuit]] in 2016 and played on the circuit through the end of 2018. She won all seven ITF singles finals she reached, ranging from the first four at the $10k to $15k level to one at the $25k level followed up by two at the $60k level. Her first three titles came at 15 years old.<ref name="itf-singles"/> The fourth was in February 2018 in her first tournament back from a seven-month layoff due to injury.<ref name=injury-2017/> After a $25k title in April, Świątek moved up to higher-level ITF events later that month. Following her junior Wimbledon title in July, she skipped the junior US Open to stay in Europe. During the two weeks of the US Open, she won back-to-back $60k titles at the [[NEK Ladies Open]] in Hungary and the [[Montreux Ladies Open]] in Switzerland.<ref name="itf-singles"/> During the second event, she defeated top seed and world No. 120, [[Mariana Duque-Mariño]].<ref name=wta-matches/><ref>{{cite web |last1=Ilic |first1=Jovica |title=17-year-old Iga Swiatek wins her second $60,000 title in Montreux |url=https://www.tennisworldusa.org/tennis/news/Rising_Stars/60180/17yearold-iga-swiatek-wins-her-second-60000-title-in-montreux/ |website=Tennis World USA |access-date=5 October 2020 |date=10 September 2018 |archive-date=12 December 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191212200618/https://www.tennisworldusa.org/tennis/news/Rising_Stars/60180/17yearold-iga-swiatek-wins-her-second-60000-title-in-montreux/ |url-status=live}}</ref> These were Świątek's last two ITF tournaments of the year.<ref name="itf-singles">{{cite web |title=Iga Swiatek Women's Singles Activity |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/iga-swiatek/800375475/pol/wt/s/activity/ |website=ITF Tennis |access-date=4 October 2020 |archive-date=22 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201022174910/https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/iga-swiatek/800375475/pol/wt/s/activity/ |url-status=live}}</ref> With these two titles, she entered the top 200 for the first time at the age of 17, rising from No. 298 to No. 180 in those two weeks.<ref name="wta-rankings">{{cite web |title=Iga Swiatek Rankings History |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/players/326408/iga-swiatek/rankings-history |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=5 October 2020 |archive-date=28 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201128101548/https://www.wtatennis.com/players/326408/iga-swiatek/rankings-history |url-status=live}}</ref>

===2019: First WTA Tour final, top 50===
[[File:Iga Swiatek forehand at 2018 Wimbledon.jpg|thumb|upright=.8|left|Świątek hitting a forehand at 2018 Wimbledon]]
Despite never playing on the WTA Tour before 2019, Świątek was able to compete in only tour-level events throughout the year. After failing to qualify at the [[2019 ASB Classic|Auckland Open]], she qualified for her first major main draw at the [[2019 Australian Open|Australian Open]]. She then defeated No. 82 [[Ana Bogdan]], in three sets, in her debut match to reach the second round. At her next three tournaments, she also qualified at the [[2019 Hungarian Ladies Open|Hungarian Ladies Open]], but not at either of the Premier Mandatory events in March.<ref name="wta-matches">{{cite web |title=Iga Swiatek Matches |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/players/326408/iga-swiatek/matches |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=5 October 2020 |archive-date=8 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201008031803/https://www.wtatennis.com/players/326408/iga-swiatek/matches |url-status=live}}</ref>

Świątek made her first WTA breakthrough at the [[2019 Ladies Open Lugano|Ladies Open Lugano]] in April. With her first direct acceptance into a main draw, she made her first WTA final. During the event, she [[upset (competition)|upset]] No. 3 seed [[Viktória Kužmová]] in the second round for her first top-50 victory.<ref>{{cite web |title=Swiatek sinks Kuzmova for first WTA QF in ===2021: Italian Open champion, top 10===
{{see also|2021 Iga Świątek tennis season}}
[[File:Swiatek RG21 (22) (51376077666).jpg|thumb|upright=.7|right|Świątek at the [[2021 French Open]]]]
At the [[2021 Australian Open – Women's singles|Australian Open]], Świątek was seeded 15th and recorded wins over [[Arantxa Rus]], [[Camila Giorgi]] and [[Fiona Ferro]]. She defeated [[Belinda Bencic]] in the final, in straight sets.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://thewest.com.au/sport/tennis/swiatek-wins-adelaide-international-ng-s-2051348 |title=Swiatek wins Adelaide International |website=Thewest.com.au |access-date=27 February 2021 |archive-date=14 April 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210414142925/https://thewest.com.au/sport/tennis/swiatek-wins-adelaide-international-ng-s-2051348 |url-status=live}}</ref> As a result, she entered the top 15 for the first time in her career, in March 2021.<ref>{{cite news |title=Rankings Watch: Adelaide title pushes Swiatek to career-high |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2049479/rankings-watch-title-pushes-swiatek-to-career-high |work=[[Women's Tennis Association|WTA Tennis]] |date=1 March 2021|access-date=25 February 2023|archive-date=25 February 2023|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230225141743/https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2049479/rankings-watch-title-pushes-swiatek-to-career-high|url-status=live}}</ref>

Seeded 15th, Świątek won her first career WTA 1000-title at the [[2021 Italian Open – Women's singles|Italian Open]], defeating former champion [[Karolína Plíšková]] in just 46 minutes without the loss of a single game. She advanced to the final after defeating two-time champion and world No. 5, [[Elina Svitolina]], and the second best-ranked teenager [[Coco Gauff]] on the same day, as well as saving match points in her third-round match against [[Barbora Krejčíková]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2143241/swiatek-staves-off-gauff-to-book-final-spot-in-rome |title=Swiatek staves off Gauff challenge to book final spot in Rome |website=Women's Tennis Association|date=15 May 2021 |access-date=2 June 2022|archive-date=11 May 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220511134644/https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2143241/swiatek-staves-off-gauff-to-book-final-spot-in-rome|url-status=live}}</ref> Świątek became the third player to win a title after saving match point en route in 2021, alongside Naomi Osaka at the Australian Open and [[Ashleigh Barty]] in Miami. She also became the fourth teenager to win a WTA 1000 event.<ref>{{cite web |date=2021 |title=Swiatek whitewashes Pliskova to capture Rome title and break Top 10 |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2145027/swiatek-whitewashes-pliskova-to-capture-rome-title-and-break-top-10|access-date=16 May 2021 |website=Women's Tennis Association|archive-date=9 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210609224730/https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2145027/swiatek-whitewashes-pliskova-to-capture-rome-title-and-break-top-10|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2021 |title=Flawless Victory: Iga Swiatek double bagels Karolina Pliskova in Rome |url=https://www.tennis.com/pro-game/2021/05/flawless-victory-iga-swiatek-double-bagels-karolina-pliskova-rome/94033/|access-date=16 May 2021 |website=tennis.com|archive-date=16 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210516142338/https://www.tennis.com/pro-game/2021/05/flawless-victory-iga-swiatek-double-bagels-karolina-pliskova-rome/94033/|url-status=live}}</ref> This successful run to her third career title moved her into the top 10 in the singles rankings on 17 May 2021, as world No. 9.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2146484/rankings-watch-swiatek-milestone-mladenovic-returns-to-doubles-no-1 |title=Rankings watch: Swiatek milestone, Mladenovic returns to Doubles No. 1 |website=Women's Tennis Association|access-date=17 May 2021|archive-date=17 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210517162943/https://www.wtatennis.com/news/2146484/rankings-watch-swiatek-milestone-mladenovic-returns-to-doubles-no-1|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Billie Jean King Cup===
Świątek made her senior [[Billie Jean King Cup]] debut in [[2018 Fed Cup|2018]] when [[Poland Fed Cup team|Poland]] was in [[2018 Fed Cup Europe/Africa Zone|Europe/Africa Zone Group I]].{{efn|The Billie Jean King Cup was known as the Fed Cup until 2020.}} To advance out of this group, Poland needed to win their round-robin group, a play-off tie against one of the other round-robin group winners, and then another play-off tie as part of the separate [[2018 Fed Cup World Group II Play-offs|World Group II Play-off]] round. They did not win their round-robin group in 2018 or [[2019 Fed Cup Europe/Africa Zone|2019]]. Świątek won her only singles match in 2018. While she won only one of her three singles matches in 2019, she won both of her doubles rubbers while partnering with [[Alicja Rosolska]]. The following year, the format was changed so that Poland needed to finish in only the top two out of three teams in their round-robin group to reach the promotional play-off. Her favourite surface is clay, having grown up playing on that surface, although she has had success on all surfaces.<ref>{{cite web |last1=McLean |first1=Ross |title=Swiatek: Winning Wimbledon Changed Everything |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/swiatek-winning-wimbledon-changed-everything/ |website=ITF Tennis |access-date=5 October 2020 |date=26 June 2019 |archive-date=31 October 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201031200935/https://www.itftennis.com/en/news-and-media/articles/swiatek-winning-wimbledon-changed-everything/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Her clay court success is enhanced by her ability to slide on the surface; as her career has progressed, she has also developed the ability to slide on hard and grass courts, too.{{Citation needed|date=June 2022}}

===Bagels and Breadsticks===
Over Świątek's WTA career up until May 2024 an average of 40.6 percent of her matches have included either a 6–0 (bagel) set or a 6–1 (breadstick) set. As a result, many have started to use the term "Iga's Bakery" to describe this.<ref name= Eccleshare>{{cite web |last= Eccleshare |first= Charlie |date= 24 May 2024 |title= Iga Swiatek's (tennis) bagels: How the WTA world No 1 bakes her 6–0 sets |url= https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5509963/2024/05/23/iga-swiatek-tennis-bagels-6-0-sets-wta/?source=nyt_sports |website= The New York Times |accessdate= 26 May 2024 |archive-date= 26 May 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240526005341/https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/5509963/2024/05/23/iga-swiatek-tennis-bagels-6-0-sets-wta/?source=nyt_sports |url-status= live }}</ref> During the 2023 season she won a bagel set in 29 percent of her matches compared with an average of 11.4 per cent of matches for matches in which she wasn't playing.<ref name= Eccleshare/> Her closest bagel rivals are Coco Gauff and Jessica Pegula, both of whom scored eight in 2023.<ref name= Eccleshare/>

As of July 2025, she has dispensed the rarer double bagel nine times in her WTA career, three of which have come during major tournaments (which were against Xinyu Wang in the 2023 French Open, against [[Anastasia Potapova]] in the 2024 French Open, and against Amanda Anisimova in the 2025 Wimbledon final).<ref>{{cite web |last= Sutelan |first= Edward |date= 7 June 2023 |title= Iga Swiatek bakery, explained: How tennis star's 'bagel & breadstick' dominance has led to meme |url= https://www.sportingnews.com/us/tennis/news/iga-swiatek-bakery-meme-tennis-bagel-breadstick/swj9fwmjuhi7rolyxku6crpy |website= Sporting News |accessdate= 27 May 2024 |archive-date= 27 May 2024 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20240527050500/https://www.sportingnews.com/us/tennis/news/iga-swiatek-bakery-meme-tennis-bagel-breadstick/swj9fwmjuhi7rolyxku6crpy |url-status= live }}</ref>

===Advocating mental health awareness===
On 10 October 2021, Świątek donated $50,000 of her prize money in support of [[World Mental Health Day]], after progressing to the last 16 of the [[Indian Wells Open]]. "I would say in sports, for me, it was always important to use that kind of help because I always thought that in my mental toughness there is some strength that I can use on court and I can also develop in that manner," she said. After winning in at the French Open in 2020, Świątek revealed that using her sports psychologist, Daria Abramowicz, had helped her get over the line. "It's just good to stay open-minded. If you need that kind of help, then go for it. If you're up to it and if you're open-minded, I think it helps a lot," Świątek added.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.cnn.com/2021/10/11/tennis/iga-swiatek-world-mental-health-day-donation-spt-intl/index.html |title=Tennis star Iga Swiatek pledges to donate $50,000 prize money to World Mental Health Day |website=[[CNN]] |first=Ben |last=Church |date=11 October 2021|access-date=2 September 2022|archive-date=7 June 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220607105334/https://www.cnn.com/2021/10/11/tennis/iga-swiatek-world-mental-health-day-donation-spt-intl/index.html|url-status=live}}</ref>

In her runner-up speech after losing the final to [[Barbora Krejčíková]] on the [[Ostrava Open]] on 9 October 2022, Świątek announced she will donate all of her prize money (€58,032) won in the tournament to Polish non-profit organizations and foundations to celebrate [[World Mental Health Day]]. "This is the most difficult moment of this tournament for me. I do not know what to say. Thank you so much. Whether I win or not, I am fulfilling my dreams, and you with me. I wish I won cause I would be able to donate more but I would like to announce that I will donate my prize money to non-profit organisations in Poland on Monday, which is [[World Mental Health Day]]. I hope this money can help a lot of people and make some change".<ref>{{cite news |url=https://polskieradio24.pl/5/4147/artykul/3050459,wta-ostrawa-wspanialy-gest-igi-swiatek-polka-zdradzila-na-co-przeznaczy-nagrode |title=Wspaniały gest Igi Świątek |website=polskieradio24.pl|archive-date=9 October 2022|access-date=9 October 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221009175255/https://polskieradio24.pl/5/4147/artykul/3050459,wta-ostrawa-wspanialy-gest-igi-swiatek-polka-zdradzila-na-co-przeznaczy-nagrode|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 12 October 2023, Świątek announced that she would donate 300,000 PLN ($70,000) to [[UNICEF]] Poland to "help in the field of mental health in Poland and all over the world", as part of celebration of [[World Mental Health Day]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.essentiallysports.com/wta-tennis-news-iga-swiatek-makes-a-seventy-thousand-dollar-commitment-to-become-the-latest-tennis-player-to-take-a-stand-for-mental-health-awareness/ |title=Iga Swiatek Makes a $70,000 Commitment to Become the Latest Tennis Player to Take a Stand for Mental Health Awareness |website=essentiallysports.com|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231106181910/https://www.essentiallysports.com/wta-tennis-news-iga-swiatek-makes-a-seventy-thousand-dollar-commitment-to-become-the-latest-tennis-player-to-take-a-stand-for-mental-health-awareness/|archive-date=6 November 2023|access-date=13 October 2023|url-status=live}}</ref> Along with the major announcement, she shared her thoughts and motivated other people as well. "I would like to encourage you to have a look around and see if there's someone there who could use your help. You can make a donation of your choice to organizations like UNICEF that professionally help people (if you're able to)."
