'''Dayana Oleksandrivna Yastremska''' ({{langx|uk|Даяна Олександрівна Ястремська}}; born 15 May 2000) is a Ukrainian professional [[tennis]] player and musician. She has been ranked as high as world No. 21 in singles by the [[Women's Tennis Association|WTA]], and No. 82 in doubles, both achieved in January 2020. Yastremska has won three [[WTA Tour]] titles. Her best [[Grand Slam (tennis)#Tournaments|major]] performance is reaching the semifinals at the [[2024 Australian Open – Women's singles|2024 Australian Open]].

A junior Grand Slam tournament runner-up in both singles and doubles, Yastremska had a quick breakthrough onto the WTA Tour. She made her debut in the top 100 and won two titles when she was 18 years old, including her first at the [[2018 Hong Kong Tennis Open|Hong Kong Open]] in 2018. She had a successful 2019 that helped her rise from No. 58 at the start of the year up to No. 22 by the end of the season. Yastremska was suspended provisionally from competition at the start of 2021 after testing positive for [[mesterolone]]. On 22 June 2021, the [[International Tennis Federation]] ruled that Yastremska was not responsible for the positive result, and that she was eligible to return to competition immediately. She made her return to the tour at the [[2021 Hamburg European Open – Women's singles|Hamburg Open]].

==Early life and background==
Dayana Yastremska was born on 15 May 2000 to Marina and Oleksandr Yastremsky in [[Odesa]], the third-largest city in Ukraine.<ref name=wta-profile/><ref name=mom-english/> She has a sister Ivanna who is six years younger.<ref name="sister">{{cite web |last1=Ubha |first1=Ravi |title=Adelaide finalist Dayana Yastremska and Sascha Bajin are dreaming big |url=https://www.tennis.com/pro-game/2020/01/dayana-yastremska-sascha-bajin-wta-adelaide/86885/ |website=Tennis.com |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 16 January 2020}}</ref> Her father had been a volleyball player and also has served on the [[Odesa City Council]]. When Yastremska was five years old, her grandfather Ivan introduced her to tennis. After trying other sports such as gymnastics and swimming, she chose to focus on tennis, saying at the age of twelve, "I chose tennis because it is very hard and beautiful. I love work and I want to write my new history in tennis." She entered her first tournament when she was seven and finished in third place, despite the event being open to children who were several years older.<ref name="bio">{{cite web |title=Хто така Даяна Ястремська: історія 18-річної зірки українського тенісу |url=https://24tv.ua/sport/dayana_yastremska_biografiya_foto_tenisistki_ukrayini_yastremskoyi_shho_vidomo_n1049368 |website=24tv.ua |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 3 February 2019 | trans-title = Who is Diana Yastremskaya: history of 18-year-old Ukrainian tennis star | language = uk | first = Igor | last = Sadienitsa}}</ref> Yastremska credits her parents for her success at tennis, saying, "When I was younger I had to sacrifice a lot of things but now I’m not regretting it... I have to say a big thank you to my parents because if they didn’t push me in the right moment then I don’t think I’d have the life I have right now."<ref name=sister/>

==Career==
===Juniors===
[[File:Dayana Yastremska Junior Orange Bowl.png|thumb|left|upright=.7|Yastremska at the 2012 Junior Orange Bowl]]
Yastremska had early success as a junior, finishing runner-up at the 12-and-under [[Junior Orange Bowl (tennis)|Junior Orange Bowl]] in 2012.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Americans Mmoh and Desiatnikov Win Junior Orange Bowl Titles; Lumsden and Oliel Give Great Britain and Israel Historic Wins |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2012/12/americans-mmoh-and-desiatnikov-win.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date = 23 December 2012}}</ref> She made her debut on the [[ITF Junior Circuit]] in March 2014 at the age of 13. Towards the end of the year, she won her first title at a Grade 4 event in November. With this success, she moved up to playing higher-level tournaments more regularly. Yastremska reached a Grade 1 semifinal in July in Austria and won a Grade 2 title in August in Hungary. She made her debut at the highest-level Grade A tournaments in October, reaching the quarterfinals at the [[Osaka Mayor's Cup]] in Japan. She fared better in doubles, finishing runner-up to two Japanese players.<ref name="itf-junior-singles">{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska Junior Singles Activity |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/dayana-yastremska/800357289/ukr/jt/s/activity |website=ITF World Tennis Tour |access-date=18 January 2020}}</ref><ref name="itf-junior-doubles">{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska Junior Doubles Activity |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/dayana-yastremska/800357289/ukr/jt/d/activity |website=ITF World Tennis Tour |access-date=18 January 2020}}</ref>

Yastremska made her junior major debut at the [[2016 Australian Open]], where she made the singles quarterfinals.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Three Australians Reach Semifinals in AO Junior Championships; Leustian, Ong Advance to Les Petits As Quarterfinals; Kingsley, Tiafoe Reach Quarterfinals in Maui $50Ks |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2016/01/three-australians-reach-semifinals-in.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date = 28 January 2016}}</ref> In doubles, she made it to the final, losing to [[Anna Kalinskaya]] and [[Tereza Mihalíková]] alongside compatriot [[Anastasia Zarycká]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Lapko, Anderson Win Australian Open Junior Titles; Leustian Reaches Les Petits As Final; Kingsley Faces McHale in Maui $50K Final; Midland Qualifying Draw Out |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2016/01/lapko-anderson-win-australian-open.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date = 30 January 2016}}</ref> Yastremska only played Grade A tournaments the rest of the year. At the [[Copa Gerdau]], she lost in the singles semifinals to [[Amanda Anisimova]]. In the doubles event, she partnered with [[Panna Udvardy]] to win the only Grade A title of her career, defeating the American team of [[Caty McNally]] and [[Natasha Subhash]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Arconada Faces Anisimova in all-US Porto Alegre Grade A Final, McNally Plays for Boys Title; Paulus Wins Grade 4 in Puerto Rico; Stewart and Min in Orlando $10K Final |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2016/03/arconada-faces-anisimova-in-all-us.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date =19 March 2016}}</ref> After a third round appearance in singles at the [[2016 French Open]],<ref name="itf-junior-singles"/> Yastremska concluded her junior career at [[2016 Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]]. At her last event, she made her only junior Grand Slam singles final. After upsetting top seed [[Olesya Pervushina]] in the semifinals,<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Potapova and Yastremska Advance to Saturday's Girls Final at Wimbledon; De Minaur and Shapovalov Reach Boys Championship Match; Three US Girls in Doubles Semifinals |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2016/07/potapova-and-yastremska-advance-to.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date = 8 July 2016}}</ref> she finished runner-up to [[Anastasia Potapova]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Lewis |first1=Colette |title=Anastasia Potapova Claims Wimbledon Girls Title in Unusual Finish; Americans Arconada, Liu and McNally Reach Girls Doubles Final |url=http://tenniskalamazoo.blogspot.com/2016/07/anastasia-potapova-claims-wimbledon.html |website=ZooTennis.com |access-date=18 January 2020 | date = 9 July 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web| title=Anastasia Potapova wins dream Wimbledon title | url=https://www.itftennis.com/juniors/news/articles/anastasia-potapova-wins-dream-wimbledon-title.aspx | last=Harwitt | first=Sandra | website=ITF Tennis | date = 9 July 2016 | access-date = 29 July 2019 | archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20190729140905/https://www.itftennis.com/juniors/news/articles/anastasia-potapova-wins-dream-wimbledon-title.aspx | url-status = dead | archive-date = 29 July 2019}}</ref> This helped her reach a career-high junior ranking of {{abbr|No.|world number}} 6 in the world.<ref>{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska Junior Singles Overview |url=https://www.itftennis.com/en/players/dayana-yastremska/800357289/ukr/jt/s/overview |website=ITF World Tennis Tour |access-date=18 January 2020}}</ref>

===2018: Hong Kong title, top 100===
[[File:Yastremska WMQ18 (25) (42646514205).jpg|thumb|upright=.7|Yastremska at the [[2018 Wimbledon Championships|2018 Wimbledon qualifying]]]]
Yastremska had a slow start into the 2018 season. She lost in the second round of qualifying at the [[2018 Australian Open|Australian Open]]. After injuring her ankle at the [[2018 Abierto Mexicano Telcel|Mexican Open]] in February,<ref>{{cite web |title=Hanging on the Dream |url=https://abiertomexicanodetenis.com/en/2018/02/27/hanging-on-the-dream/ |website=Abierto Mexicano de Tennis |access-date=1 March 2020 | date = 27 February 2018}}</ref> she did not enter any tournaments in March. With a ranking well outside of the top 100, she needed to enter qualifying at her first seven tournaments of the year, reaching three main draws.<ref name=itf-singles/> At the last of these events, Yastremska produced her best result, finishing runner-up to [[Rebecca Peterson]] at the $100k [[2018 Open de Cagnes-sur-Mer|Open de Cagnes-sur-Mer]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Petersons stora succé – är nu bäst i Sverige |url=https://www.expressen.se/sport/tennis/petersons-stora-succe-ar-nu-bast-i-sverige/ |website=Expressen |access-date=19 January 2020 | language = sv | date = 13 May 2018 |first= Linus | last = Sunnervik| trans-title = Peterson's great success - is now the best in Sweden}}</ref> This result took her into the top 150 for the first time.<ref name=wta-rankings/> She also reached the final at the $100k [[2018 Fuzion 100 Ilkley Trophy|Ilkley Trophy]], losing to [[Tereza Smitková]] in a third-set tiebreak.<ref>{{cite web |title=Fuzion 100 Ilkley Trophy: Smitkova and Stakhovsky crowned singles champions |url=https://www.lta.org.uk/major-tennis-events/trophy-series/ilkley-trophy/news/2018/fuzion-100-ilkley-trophy-smitkova-and-stakhovsky-crowned-singles-champions/# |website=Lawn Tennis Association |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 24 June 2018}}</ref> She did not enter the [[2018 French Open|French Open]] and lost in the qualifying competition at [[2018 Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]].<ref name=itf-singles/> During the second week of Wimbledon, Yastremska won the separate $60k [[2018 Torneo Internazionale Femminile Antico Tiro a Volo|Internazionale Antico Tiro a Volo]] in Rome. She defeated Potapova in the final in 45 minutes, only losing one game.<ref>{{cite web| url=https://www.tennisworldusa.org/tennis/news/Rising_Stars/57510/dayana-yastremska-thumps-anastasia-potapova-in-rome-for-the-third-pro-title/| title=Dayana Yastremska thumps Anastasia Potapova in Rome for the third pro title | website=Tennis World USA | access-date=25 November 2018 | first = Jovaca | last = Ilic | date = 9 July 2018}}</ref> With this title, she made her debut in the top 100 at the age of 18.<ref name=wta-rankings/><ref>{{cite web| url=http://www.wtatennis.com/news/ukrainian-teen-yastremska-first-new-millennium-wta-top-100 | title=Ukrainian teen Yastremska first of new millennium into WTA Top 100 | last = Kane | first = David | website=WTA Tennis | date = 17 July 2018 | access-date = 17 July 2018}}</ref>

In the second half of the season, Yastremska had more success at the WTA level. She qualified for the Premier-level [[2018 Connecticut Open|Connecticut Open]] where she upset No. 36, [[Danielle Collins]], for her only main-draw match win.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Fornabaio |first1=Michael |title=Connecticut Open: Yastremska making name for self, tops final American in draw |url=https://www.nhregister.com/sports/article/Connecticut-Open-Yastremska-making-name-for-13169827.php |website=New Haven Register |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 20 August 2018}}</ref> The following week, she made her Grand Slam main-draw debut and was upset in her opening-round match by qualifier [[Karolína Muchová]].<ref name="wta-matches">{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska Matches |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/players/324261/dayana-yastremska/matches |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020}}</ref> Yastremska made her breakthrough on the tour in her last two tournaments of the year. In October, she won her maiden WTA Tour title at the [[2018 Hong Kong Tennis Open|Hong Kong Open]] without dropping a set. She defeated three top 100 Chinese players in the event, including No. 40 [[Zhang Shuai]] and No. 24 [[Wang Qiang (tennis)|Wang Qiang]] in the semifinals and final, respectively.<ref name=first-title>{{cite web| url=http://www.wtatennis.com/news/teenager-yastremska-zooms-first-wta-singles-title-career-hong-kong | title=Teenager Yastremska zooms to first WTA singles title of career in Hong Kong | last=Juzwiak | first=Jason | date = 14 October 2018 | website=WTA Tennis | access-date = 25 November 2018}}</ref> The following week, she returned to Europe and reached another semifinal at the [[2018 BGL Luxembourg Open|Luxembourg Open]]. She recorded her first career top-20 victory against No. 13, [[Garbiñe Muguruza]], before losing to [[Belinda Bencic]] in a third-set tiebreak.<ref>{{cite web| url=http://www.wtatennis.com/news/yastremska-stuns-muguruza-career-best-win-luxembourg | title=Yastremska stuns Muguruza for career-best win in Luxembourg | last=Livaudais | first=Stephanie | date = 17 October 2018 | website=WTA Tennis | access-date = 25 November 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite web| url=http://www.wtatennis.com/news/bencic-outlasts-yastremska-epic-reach-luxembourg-final | title=Bencic outlasts Yastremska in epic to reach Luxembourg final | last=Juzwiak | first=Jason | date = 19 October 2018 | website=WTA Tennis | access-date = 25 November 2018}}</ref> With these two results, she rose from No. 110 at the start of the month to No. 58 by the end of the year.<ref name=wta-rankings/>

===2019: Two WTA titles, top 25===
Yastremska continued to rise in the WTA rankings throughout the season, her first full year on the WTA Tour. After a quarterfinal appearance at the [[2019 Hobart International|Hobart International]], she won her first two Grand Slam main draw matches at the [[2019 Australian Open|Australian Open]] before losing to [[Serena Williams]] in the third round.<ref>{{cite web |title=Serena stops Yastremska to reach Australian Open fourth round |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1450741/serena-stops-yastremska-to-reach-australian-open-fourth-round |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 19 January 2019}}</ref> At her next tournament, she won her second WTA Tour title at the [[2019 Hua Hin Championships|Hua Hin Championships]] in Thailand. She upset top seed Muguruza again in the quarterfinal, and then defeated [[Ajla Tomljanović]] in the final in a third-set tiebreak.<ref name=second-title>{{cite web| title=Yastremska outlasts Tomljanovic to win second career title in Hua Hin | url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/yastremska-outlasts-tomljanovic-win-second-career-title-hua-hin | website=WTA Tennis | date = 2 February 2019 | access-date = 2 February 2019}}</ref> This title brought Yastremska to No. 34 in the world.<ref name=wta-rankings/> Following this title, however, she began to struggle and only tallied one match win in her next six tournaments in part due to playing through an ankle injury.<ref name="mom-english">{{cite web |last1=Livaudais |first1=Stephanie |title='It was really hard to play': Dayana Yastremska on rising past family trauma |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1446277/-it-was-really-hard-to-play-dayana-yastremska-on-rising-past-family-trauma |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 8 August 2019}}</ref><ref name=wta-matches/>

In late May, Yastremska recovered to win another title at the [[2019 Internationaux de Strasbourg|Internationaux de Strasbourg]]. She upset No. 11, [[Aryna Sabalenka]], in the semifinals and then defeated No. 24, [[Caroline Garcia]], in another third-set tiebreak in a nearly three-hour match for the title.<ref name=third-title>{{cite web| title=Yastremska beats Garcia in longest final of the season for third career title in Strasbourg | url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/yastremska-beats-garcia-longest-final-season-third-career-title-strasbourg | website=WTA Tennis | date = 25 May 2019 | access-date = 1 July 2019}}</ref>

While she lost her opening-round match at the [[2019 French Open|French Open]] to [[Carla Suárez Navarro]],<ref>{{cite web |last1=Kane |first1=David |title=Suarez Navarro survives Strasbourg champion Yastremska in Paris |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1448002/suarez-navarro-survives-strasbourg-champion-yastremska-in-paris |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date =27 May 2019}}</ref> she reached the fourth round at [[2019 Wimbledon Championships|Wimbledon]], her best Grand Slam result to date. She upset No. 28 [[Sofia Kenin]] before losing to Zhang Shuai.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Macpherson |first1=Alex |title=Yastremska takes control to upset Kenin in youthful Wimbledon thriller |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1447081/yastremska-takes-control-to-upset-kenin-in-youthful-wimbledon-thriller |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 3 July 2019}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Zhang downs teen Yastremska to reach Wimbledon QF |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1446949/zhang-downs-teen-yastremska-to-reach-wimbledon-qf |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 8 July 2019}}</ref>

In the second half of the season, Yastremska produced another good performance at a major at the [[2019 US Open (tennis)|US Open]], losing in the third round to compatriot and world No. 5, [[Elina Svitolina]].<ref>{{cite web |last1=Kapetanakis |first1=Arthur |title=Svitolina dominates Yastremska in Ukrainian showdown at 2019 US Open |url=https://www.usopen.org/en_US/news/articles/2019-08-30/2019-08-30_svitolina_dominates_yastremska_in_ukrainian_showdown_at_2019_us_open.html |website=US Open |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 30 August 2019}}</ref> She then achieved her best Premier 5 result of the year, a quarterfinal at the [[2019 Wuhan Open|Wuhan Open]]. ===2020: First Premier final===
Yastremska had a strong start into the 2020 season. She reached the final at the Premier-level [[2020 Adelaide International|Adelaide International]]. During the event, she defeated three top-20 players, including No. 12 Aryna Sabalenka, before finishing runner-up to world No. 1, [[Ashleigh Barty]], who won the title in her home country.<ref>{{cite web |title=Days before Australian Open, world No. 1 Barty wins Adelaide title |url=https://www.tennis.com/pro-game/2020/01/top-ranked-barty-wins-1st-title-at-home-ahead-of-aussie-open/86911/ |website=Tennis.com |access-date=19 January 2020| date=18 January 2020 | agency = Associated Press}}</ref> At the [[2020 Australian Open – Women's singles|Australian Open]], she beat [[Kaja Juvan]] in the first round, before falling to [[Caroline Wozniacki]] in the second, the Dane's final tournament victory before her retirement.

[[Ukraine Fed Cup team|Ukraine]] played their [[2020 Fed Cup Europe/Africa Zone Group I – Pool A (Tallinn)|Fed Cup]] Group I tournament in [[Tallinn|Estonia]], where Yastremska was victorious in all three of her singles matches. She then lost in the first round in [[2020 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles|Dubai]] to [[Veronika Kudermetova]], before falling to [[Garbiñe Muguruza]] in the third round in [[2020 Qatar Total Open – Singles|Doha]]. This was her last match before international tennis was suspended due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].

International tennis resumed in [[2020 Internazionali Femminili di Palermo – Singles|Palermo]] in August, where Yastremska lost to [[Camila Giorgi]] in the singles quarterfinals. Moving to New York, she lost to [[Naomi Osaka]] in the third round of the relocated [[2020 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles|Cincinnati]] Premier-5 tournament before falling to [[Madison Brengle]] in the second round of the [[2020 US Open – Women's singles|US Open]]. Returning to Europe, she lost to [[Simona Halep]] in the third round of the [[2020 Italian Open – Women's singles|Italian Open]] and, in a big upset, to [[Daria Gavrilova]] in the first round of the [[2020 French Open – Women's singles|French Open]].

First-round losses in [[2020 J&T Banka Ostrava Open – Singles|Ostrava]] and [[2020 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Singles|Linz]] completed a disappointing end to the season, one in which her only success in doubles was a win in the [[2020 Fed Cup Europe/Africa Zone Group I – Pool A (Tallinn)|Fed Cup]] tie against [[Estonia Fed Cup team|Estonia]]. She lost in the first round of all six tournaments she played.

===2021: Provisional doping suspension===
On 7 January 2021, the [[International Tennis Federation|ITF]] announced that, in November 2020, Yastremska had tested positive for a metabolite of [[mesterolone]], an anabolic steroid medication which is prohibited by [[World Anti-Doping Agency|WADA]]. The ITF announced that, as a result of testing positive for this banned substance, Yastremska shall be provisionally suspended, "pending determination of the charge against her at a full hearing".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/317960.aspx|title=Dayana Yastremska|work=[[International Tennis Federation]] Anti-Doping|date=7 January 2021|access-date=7 January 2021|archive-date=8 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210108041423/https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/317960.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref> That same day, Yastremska released a statement on Twitter, in which she denied the charges against her, asserting that she "[has] never used any performance-enhancing drugs or any prohibited substances", and that she believes the positive result to be as a result of contamination. She further stated that she and her team are "resolutely determined to do everything to clear [her] name".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/D_Yastremska/status/1347199074626396161|title=Dayana Yastremska on Twitter|work=[[Twitter]]|date=7 January 2021|access-date=7 January 2021}}</ref> After travelling to [[Melbourne]], she applied for her suspension by the ITF to be lifted; this request was denied by an independent tribunal on 23 January.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/317992.aspx|title=Application by Dayana Yastremska to lift Provisional Suspension denied|work=[[International Tennis Federation]] Anti-Doping|date=23 February 2021|access-date=23 January 2021|archive-date=23 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210123193344/https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/317992.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref> Her appeal against a doping ban was fast-tracked in order for her case to be resolved before the Australian Open, which began on 8 February 2021.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news|title=Yastremska doping appeal fast-tracked|language=en-GB|work=BBC Sport|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/tennis/55799814|access-date=2021-01-26}}</ref> The [[Court of Arbitration for Sport|CAS]] said it would make a decision by 3 February 2021.<ref name=":0" /> On that date, the CAS announced that it had dismissed her appeal, and that the provisional suspension enforced by the ITF on 7 January would be upheld until the ITF reached a final decision.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.tas-cas.org/fileadmin/user_upload/CAS_Media_Release_7657_decision.pdf|title=Media Release, Tennis Anti-Doping: The Court of Arbitration of Sport (CAS) dismisses the appeal filed by Dayana Yastremska: Provisional suspension maintained|work=[[Court of Arbitration for Sport]]|date=3 February 2020|access-date=3 February 2020}}</ref> After the CAS released their statement, Yastremska acknowledged the provisional suspension, but said that she couldn't "express [her] disappointment about not being able to take part in the first Slam of the year" due to her suspension. She also stated that she is "confident" that she and her team will "prove [her] innocence". She vowed to "clear my name".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/D_Yastremska/status/1357016745798754306|title=Dayana Yastremska on Twitter|work=[[Twitter]]|date=3 February 2020|access-date=3 February 2020}}</ref> The CAS announced on 27 April 2021 that a second application filed by Yastremska to lift her suspension had been denied,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/318940.aspx|title=Second application by Dayana Yastremska to lift Provisional Suspension denied|date=27 April 2021|access-date=17 May 2021|archive-date=10 February 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220210212118/https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/318940.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref> and, on 7 May 2021, they announced that an appeal filed by Yastremska against the decision to not lift her provisional suspension had been dismissed, and that she would be ineligible for competition, "pending the final resolution of her case".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/318965.aspx|title=Second appeal to CAS by Dayana Yastremska against decision to not lift Provisional Suspension dismissed|work=[[International Tennis Federation]] Anti-Doping|date=7 May 2021|access-date=17 May 2021|archive-date=21 April 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220421090651/https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/318965.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref>

On 22 June 2021, the ITF ruled that Yastremska "bore no fault or negligence" for the positive test result, and announced that her provisional suspension would be lifted with immediate effect. She would serve no period of ineligibility, and would be able to resume competition immediately.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/319114.aspx|title=Decision in the case of Dayana Yastremska|work=[[International Tennis Federation]] Anti-Doping|date=22 June 2021|access-date=22 June 2021|archive-date=21 April 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220421090342/https://antidoping.itftennis.com/news/319114.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref> Due to the late decision by the ITF, Yastremska was ineligible to compete at [[2021 Wimbledon Championships – Women's singles|Wimbledon]]; as such, Yastremska returned to the tour at [[2021 Hamburg European Open – Women's singles|Hamburg]]. Playing as the top seed, she defeated [[Magdalena Fręch]] and [[Sara Errani]], before being defeated by the qualifier and eventual champion [[Elena-Gabriela Ruse]]. Her next tournament was at the [[Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Tokyo Olympics]], where she suffered two first-round defeats: to [[Leylah Fernandez]] in the [[Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Women's singles|singles event]], and, partnering Elina Svitolina, to [[Alizé Cornet]] and [[Fiona Ferro]] of France in the [[Tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Women's doubles|doubles competition]].

Her U.S. and Canadian tournaments saw little success with first-round losses at the [[2021 Silicon Valley Classic – Singles|Silicon Valley Classic]], [[2021 National Bank Open – Women's singles|Canadian Open]], and [[2021 US Open – Women's singles|US Open]] and second-round losses at the [[2021 Western & Southern Open – Women's singles|San Diego Open]], [[2021 Chicago Fall Tennis Classic – Singles|Chicago Fall Tennis Classic]], and [[2021 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles|Indian Wells Open]] to eventual champion [[Paula Badosa]]. She retired in the first round at [[2021 Zavarovalnica Sava Portorož – Singles|Portorož]] against eventual champion [[Jasmine Paolini]]. After losing in the first round at the [[2021 Kremlin Cup – Women's singles|Kremlin Cup]], she reached the quarterfinals at the [[2021 Courmayeur Ladies Open – Singles|Courmayeur Ladies Open]] in a rematch against Paolini and lost in straight sets. Yastremska concluded her season with another rematch and loss against Paolini at the [[2021 Upper Austria Ladies Linz – Singles|Upper Austria Ladies Linz]]. She finished year ranked inside the top 100 at 97.

===2022: WTA 250 final, top 100===
[[File:2022 Wimbledon 1st round 2022-06-65 (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=.7|Yastremska in a doubles action along with [[Dalma Gálfi]] (back) at the [[2022 Wimbledon Championships]]]]
The beginning of the season started with Yastremska's first season opener in Australia since 2020. She was eliminated in the first round of qualifying at the [[2022 Melbourne Summer Set 1 – Women's singles|Melbourne Summer Set 1]] and in the first round of the main draw at the [[2022 Adelaide International 2 – Women's singles|Adelaide International 2]]. Her Australian trip concluded with a retirement against [[Madison Brengle]] in the opening round at the [[2022 Australian Open – Women's singles|Australian Open]]. Her ranking dropped to 150 at the conclusion of the Australian Open.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.wtatennis.com/players/324261/dayana-yastremska/rankings-history | title=Dayana Yastremska &#124; Player Stats & More – WTA Official }}</ref>

Yastremska entered the main draw of the [[2022 Dubai Tennis Championships – Women's singles|Dubai Championships]] as a qualifier, after winning three qualifying matches with the last a rematch against her Australian Open opponent Madison Brengle. There she reached the quarterfinals where she was defeated by [[Markéta Vondroušová]]. Shortly after the conclusion of this tournament marked the beginning of the [[2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine|Russian invasion of Ukraine]]. Yastremska was living with her family in [[Odesa, Ukraine]] at the time of the invasion and was able to seek refuge in [[France]] along with her 15-year-old sister Ivanna Yastremska.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.espn.com/tennis/story/_/id/33376348/ukrainian-tennis-player-dayana-yastremska-flees-country-arrives-safely-france | title=Tennis player Dayana Yastremska flees Ukraine, arrives safely in France | date=26 February 2022 }}</ref> The two received a wildcard entry for the [[2022 WTA Lyon Open|Lyon Open]] where Yastremska was able to reach her first final since 2020. However, she was defeated by Zhang Shuai, in three sets. She accepted a wildcard entry into the [[2022 BNP Paribas Open – Women's singles|Indian Wells Open]] and was defeated by former world No. 4, Caroline Garcia, in the first round. Her "Sunshine Double" (Miami and Indian Wells Opens) concluded with a retirement in the first round of qualifying in the [[2022 Miami Open – Women's singles|Miami Open]]. After reaching the quarterfinals of the [[2022 Copa Colsanitas – Singles|Copa Colsanitas]], she reentered the top 100 in the singles rankings.

She played as [[Ukraine Billie Jean King Cup team|Ukraine's]] top seed in the [[2022 Billie Jean King Cup qualifying round|Billie Jean King Cup qualifiers]], in a tie against the [[United States Billie Jean King Cup team|United States]]. She defeated [[Jessica Pegula]] after having lost to [[Alison Riske]] in her first match of the qualifying tournament. Her clay-court season continued after the Copa Colsanitas by qualifying for the [[2022 Mutua Madrid Open – Women's singles|Madrid Open]]. She was defeated in the second round by [[Marie Bouzková]].

==Playing style==
[[File:Dayana Yastremska (2023 US Open) 02 (cropped).jpg|thumb|left|upright=.7|Yastremska swinging a forehand]]
Her former coach [[Sascha Bajin]] described Yastremska in the following terms: "She’s a great mover and she has incredible power. Her ground strokes are very powerful."<ref name=sister/> Yastremska is an aggressive baseline player, whose attacking style allows her to hit a large number of winners - she hit 33 winners in her first two finals, and 49 winners when she won her third title. Her highly aggressive playing style also lends itself to a high unforced error count, with Yastremska hitting 21, 53, and 50 unforced errors in her three final matches.<ref name=first-title/><ref name=second-title/><ref name=third-title/> As of the 2019 Australian Open, Yastremska was ranked as the most aggressive player on the WTA Tour, having led the tour with 28.6 per cent of her shots ending in a winner, an unforced error, or an opponent's forced error; this statistic is known as the Aggression Score.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Sackmann |first1=Jeff |title=Dayana Yastremska Hits Harder Than You |url=http://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2019/01/18/dayana-yastremska-hits-harder-than-you/ |website=Tennis Abstract |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 18 January 2019}}</ref><ref name=economist>{{cite news |title=Sloane Stephens shows you don't have to be aggressive to win a major |url=https://www.economist.com/game-theory/2017/09/12/sloane-stephens-shows-you-dont-have-to-be-aggressive-to-win-a-major |newspaper=The Economist |access-date=4 July 2018 | date = 12 September 2017 | first = Jeff | last = Sackmann}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |title=Measuring WTA Tactics With Aggression Score |url=http://www.tennisabstract.com/blog/2015/08/31/measuring-wta-tactics-with-aggression-score/ |website=Tennis Abstract|access-date= 4 July 2018 | date = 31 August 2015 | first = Jeff | last = Sackmann}}</ref> Yastremska's groundstrokes are hit with relentless power, depth, and speed, with her forehand being her best shot, due to the extreme amount of racquet head speed she creates. She is capable of winning a high percentage of points when she hits an inside-in forehand in particular, and she can hit winners from any position of the court with her forehand.<ref name="profiler">{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska |url=https://www.tennisprofiler.com/wtaplayer/Yastremska-Dayana |website=Tennis Profiler |access-date=19 September 2020}}</ref> Despite this, Yastremska also possesses an incredibly powerful backhand, which is typically hit flat, allowing her to hit sharply angled winners. She can also strategically apply slice to her backhand, allowing her to break up the pace of rallies and construct points intelligently. Her average first serve speed is about {{convert|100|mph}}, although her first serve speed can scale 116&nbsp;mph (187&nbsp;km/h), allowing her to serve numerous aces in any match.<ref>{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska |url=http://www.tennisabstract.com/cgi-bin/wplayer.cgi?p=DayanaYastremska |website=Tennis Abstract |access-date=19 January 2020}}</ref> Although she ranked in the top 20 on the WTA Tour in aces in 2019, she also ranked in the top 10 in double faults, as she tends to take risks on her second serve.<ref name="wta-statistics-2019">{{cite web |title=Statistics |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/stats/2019 |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 September 2020}}</ref>

==Coaches==
Yastremska's parents have both been a part of her coaching team,<ref name="neva-cup-interview">{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska: "If we play with my sister, we will be better than Serena and Venus" |url=http://nevacup.formulatx.com/en/2017/09/22/dayana-yastremskaya-esli-budem-igrat-paru-s-sestroj-budem-luchshe-chem-serena-i-vinus/ |website=Neva Cup |access-date=19 January 2020 |date=22 September 2017 |archive-date=19 January 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200119073939/http://nevacup.formulatx.com/en/2017/09/22/dayana-yastremskaya-esli-budem-igrat-paru-s-sestroj-budem-luchshe-chem-serena-i-vinus/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> with her mother continuing to serve as her mental coach.<ref name=mom-english/> When Yastremska was a junior, she had a variety of different coaches. She worked with former top 100 player [[Viktoriya Kutuzova]] and Kutuzova's father Valery. She later worked for few months with another former top 100 player in [[Jean-René Lisnard]], then with Bastien Fazincani, and then with the former world No. 2 [[Magnus Norman]].<ref name=bio/> As a professional in 2017, she has trained in Istanbul with Gavin Hopper, a former coach of [[Monica Seles]].<ref name="kane-2018">{{cite web |last1=Kane |first1=David |date= 19 December 2017 |title=2018 Scouting Report: Why young gun Dayana Yastremska is ready for primetime |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1414761/2018-scouting-report-why-young-gun-dayana-yastremska-is-ready-for-primetime |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020}}</ref> Yastremska later began training at the Justine Henin Academy, with [[Justine Henin|Henin]] becoming her consultant in 2018. She worked with one of the academy's coaches, Oliver Jeunehomme, until September 2019.<ref>{{cite web |title=Dayana Yastremska splits with coach Oliver Jeunehomme |url=https://www.tennis.com/pro-game/2019/09/dayana-yastremska-splits-coach-oliver-jeunehomme-justine-henin-academy/85212/ |website=Tennis.com |access-date=19 January 2020 | first= Kamakshi | last = Tandon | date = 28 September 2019}}</ref> In the offseason, she hired [[Sascha Bajin]] as her new coach.<ref>{{cite web |title=Yastremska announces Bajin as new coach for 2020 |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1498182/yastremska-announces-bajin-as-new-coach-for-2020 |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 January 2020 | date = 21 November 2019}}</ref> In September 2020 they split after Yastremska had a disappointing performance in the [[2020 US Open (tennis)|2020 US Open]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1820488/yastremska-reveals-bajin-split-following-us-open | title=Yastremska reveals Bajin split following US Open }}</ref> She was then coached by [[Dorian Descloix]] after he split with Monica Puig in 2022.

She is currently coached by Emmanuel Heussner at the Myteam Tennis Center near Nice, south of France.
Marcis Garuts is traveling with her as hitting partner.

==Personal life==
At the [[2019 Australian Open]], Yastremska's mother suffered an eye injury after a champagne bottle that she was holding exploded unexpectedly. She had immediate surgery to save her eye, which was coordinated in part by [[Elina Svitolina]]'s agent Stefan Gurov and financially covered by tournament director Craig Tiley. Yastremska dedicated her title at the [[2019 Hua Hin Championships|Hua Hin Championships]] the following month to her mother as a result of this incident.<ref name=mom-english/>

During the [[COVID-19 pandemic]] in 2020, Yastremska began a music career. She released her first single, "Thousands of Me", in May, and a dance song, "Favourite Track", in August.<ref>{{cite web |title=Kvitova back on court, Yastremska's debut single |url=https://www.wtatennis.com/news/1668167/kvitova-back-on-court-yastremska-s-debut-single |website=WTA Tennis |access-date=19 September 2020 | date = 13 May 2020}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Savage |first1=Nic |title=Tennis: Dayana Yastremska becomes a star away from the court in the music studio |url=https://www.news.com.au/sport/sports-life/tennis-dayana-yastremska-becomes-a-star-away-from-the-court-in-the-music-studio/news-story/eca9449f2adfe96c4a1eb4bb55dd4815 |website=news.com.au |access-date=19 September 2020 | date = 2 August 2020}}</ref>

On 9 July 2020, in the wake of the [[murder of George Floyd]] in [[Minneapolis]], Minnesota, world-wide protests, and increased visibility of the [[Black Lives Matter]] movement, Yastremska posted four images of herself to her Twitter and Instagram accounts of her sporting half-white, half-black makeup, with the caption "Equality". Yastremska was widely criticised on social media, with many pointing out that her sporting [[blackface]] was antithetical to the message she was attempting to convey. Yastremska deleted the posts, and apologised "to those that [she had] offended", claiming that she had been "misunderstood", and denied that she was wearing blackface.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://au.sports.yahoo.com/tennis-dayana-yastremska-apologises-blackface-stunt-005615776.html|title="Lost the plot": Tennis player's 'blackface' stunt sparks furore|work=[[Yahoo! Sports]]|last1=Morgan|first1=Riley|date=10 July 2020|accessdate=25 January 2021}}</ref>

During the [[2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine]], Yastremska fled to France for safety, alongside her younger sister, Ivanna, on 26 February, being forced to leave her parents behind in her hometown of [[Odesa]].<ref name="Reuters">{{cite web|url=https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/sports/ukrainian-tennis-player-yastremska-flees-safety-france-2022-02-27/|title=Ukrainian tennis player Yastremska flees to safety in France|work=[[Reuters]]|last1=Carroll|first1=Rory|date=27 February 2022|accessdate=27 February 2022}}</ref>

===Accusations of gamesmanship===
Yastremska has been accused of [[gamesmanship]] by commentators and fellow players, and for allegedly exaggerating and faking injuries to exploit the medical time-out system. One such player was former world No. 1, [[Caroline Wozniacki]], following their second-round match at the [[2020 Australian Open]], where Yastremska took multiple medical time-outs throughout the match. Wozniacki stated in a post-match press conference that she "didn't think there was anything wrong" with Yastremska, and that "it's a trick she's done before, and [Wozniacki] knew it was coming", designed to "break [Wozniacki's] rhythm". She was widely criticised for doing so in the final at Hua Hin, as, trailing 5–2 in the third set against [[Ajla Tomljanović]], she took three medical time-outs for a non-specific injury, rallying to win the third set, 7–6.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.smh.com.au/sport/tomljanovic-loses-to-talented-teen-in-controversial-thai-open-final-20190204-p50vgn.html|title=Tomljanovic loses to talented teen in controversial Thai Open final|work=[[Sydney Morning Herald]]|date=4 February 2019|accessdate=25 January 2021}}</ref> Yastremska has denied the allegations that she fakes injuries to exploit the medical time-out system, claiming that she only takes medical time-outs when she feels she needs them.
